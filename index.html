Playing well is measurable

A bot that fills the stations at random earns a star plate on 10.6% of courses. One that reads the board before it assigns earns 66.4%. Both figures come from 500 simulated seasons, and the tests refuse the build if that gap moves by more than three points.

02

The bar rises to meet you

Every plate is scored against one number, and that number climbs with your reputation. There is no difficulty setting, because the difficulty is the score.

03

Nothing is hidden from you

Lean on a station and the game shows the odds first — star plate 4% → 14%, defect 13% → 26% — and only then charges you the wear. No rubber-banding, nothing rounded in your favour, and the guest's tells were checked against 100,000 evenings.

What it is not

  • No graphics. Text, numbers, and one brass colour.
  • Nothing real-time. You decide, then watch the evening play back.
  • Rough in places — it plays end to end, and I promise nothing beyond that.
  • One person. Czech and English, switchable mid-game.

I want playtesters, not applause

Stop the moment it stops being interesting. Where you stopped tells me more than a compliment does — and it is the one thing I cannot find out on my own.

  1. Before your first Start service — what did you think was going to go wrong?
  2. Which evening were you on when you stopped, and what were you doing?
  3. Send the kitchen code. It replays the exact season you played.
